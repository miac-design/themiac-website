Program outcomes reported in Austin AI Hub’s August 2026 impact report.

The project

A global challenge needed a website, a clear path from registration to submission, and a team to bring the program and awards event together.

My role

I was the lead producer of the hackathon, carrying the program from initiation through execution. My work connected the website, program design, partnerships, outreach, participant communications, judging workflows and awards event. I built the hackathon website in full and worked directly with Call for Code AI on announcement reviews and public communications.

  • Built the entire hackathon website, giving participants a place to understand the challenge, register and find program information.
  • Designed the operational blueprint, including challenge milestones, submission screening, judging workflows and mentor support.
  • Led partnership coordination, community outreach, event promotion and participant communications across the program.
  • Led event planning and production through the awards ceremony, bringing the program team, partners and community together.

Design and delivery choices

The participant journey brings registration, challenge tracks, workshop resources and submission guidance into one website. The operational blueprint uses a screening stage before panel judging and a shared support channel to organize participant questions. These are concrete parts of the program design, rather than a website alone.

I am quoted in both published Call for Code AI announcements: the April 30 launch and the July 30 winners announcement. The launch release also lists me as an Austin AI Hub media contact. The winners announcement reports participation from dozens of countries across six continents. The impact report identifies five teams selected for a six-month incubator, with the remaining honored teams invited to join. The accelerator is the next phase of the work and remains underway.

GEO implementation

Making the organization easier to find

The project

The organization needed clear, consistent information that search engines and AI assistants could read and verify.

My role

I worked on the website and its generative engine optimization, combining an audit of the organization’s online presence with content and technical changes.

  • Standardized organization details and corrected inconsistent information.
  • Worked with JSON-LD structured data, including organization and FAQ content.
  • Addressed crawler access through robots.txt, alongside sitemap and canonical-tag work.
  • Developed factual About content and direct-answer FAQs.
  • Reviewed external entity profiles and authority signals.

Outcome and context

This work strengthened the site’s content and machine-readable organization information. AI-citation and traffic lift have not yet been measured with a comparable baseline. The work was pro bono, and Austin AI Hub and EDRON AI share leadership.
